This document compares SwarajKavach — the Next-Gen ESDSWAF platform — against the five vendors that appear on every industry shortlist. It is written to be checkable: every capability claimed for ESDSWAF is marked by whether it ships and has been tested, is partial, or is roadmap. Nothing is listed as delivered on the strength of a design document.

Why the marks matter An internal audit on 13 August 2026 checked our own customer deck against the source tree and found six capabilities claimed as shipped that the code did not support. All six were corrected. This assessment applies the same standard from the outset — which is why several rows below are deliberately less flattering than earlier material.

1Market position — the comparison that is actually ours to win

The five recognised leaders are Cloudflare, Akamai and Imperva (Gartner Leaders in the Cloud WAAP Magic Quadrant), with AWS WAF as a Challenger and F5 holding regulated enterprise. The market is fragmented — no vendor exceeds 15% share, so there is no dominant incumbent to displace.

Four of those five are global anycast edge platforms. Competing with them on edge footprint would mean building a multi-terabit CDN, and any comparison framed that way is one we lose on capital, not on engineering. The segment ESDSWAF addresses is the one they structurally cannot serve:

  • Data that must remain on-premise and in-country — the binding constraint for Indian BFSI, government and healthcare, and the reason F5 and Fortinet still hold regulated verticals.
  • Delivered by the hosting provider — the customer's workload is already in an ESDS datacenter. That is a distribution advantage none of the five have with our customers.
  • Single-tenant and customer-dedicated — dedicated inspection, not a shared multi-tenant edge.
  • Fully air-gapped operation — installs and runs with no outbound internet dependency.

Our real competitive set is therefore F5 and Imperva, plus in India Indusface AppTrana, HaltDOS and our own eNlight Pratihar.

How the detection figure was reached — and why the path matters

The detection score was not achieved by tuning thresholds. It came from finding four places where a component was judging data it could not see. That history is worth a CTO's attention because it is the difference between a number that holds and one that collapses on a new corpus.

4Honest scorecard

Where we genuinely lead

  • Onboarding speed — API or YAML, live in seconds, zero restart. F5 needs policy plus virtual-server attach; HaltDOS is console-driven.
  • Truthful enforcement status — the console reports which rules are actually running on each node. No competitor offers this.
  • Measured evasion resistance — 99.09% app true-positive. Neither F5 nor HaltDOS publishes a comparable figure.
  • Modern API security in one policy — GraphQL, JWKS and gRPC together.
  • Sandboxed WASM plugins in any language, hot-swappable.
  • India PII in DLP — PAN and Aadhaar built in.
  • Air-gapped single-tenant deployment with data residency by construction.

Where we trail — do not claim parity

Every item below, plus the operational defects behind them, is tracked with evidence in FIXES-TO-BE-MADE.md at the repository root.

  • Partial CRS rule compatibility. About half the shipped ruleset loads; ModSecurity-based products (BunkerWeb, and anything on libmodsecurity) run it whole. Our detection score is higher than theirs, but the coverage claim is not ours to make yet.
  • No positive-security / entity-learning model. This is F5's core differentiator and our API discovery is a toggle only.
  • 5 response actions vs HaltDOS's 13 — no tarpit, custom response or redirect.
  • Fewer authorable rule categories than HaltDOS.
  • Rule authoring UI withheld — the validator does not parse our real grammar yet.
  • No global scrubbing network — and no roadmap to one.
  • No third-party audit, red team or compliance attestation. A procurement blocker independent of engineering quality.
  • No vendor threat-research team.
  • Real-traffic false-positive rate unmeasured. See §6.

For reference, a coverage audit against the eNlight Pratihar WAAP reference specification assessed 35 covered, 36 partial, 43 gaps across roughly 114 functionalities. The core engine scored strongly; API security was the weakest domain and has since been the focus of two build phases.

6The gap that matters most — and it is not a feature

Every detection figure in §3 comes from a synthetic corpus. GoTestWAF measures whether we catch known attack classes and whether we pass its benign samples. It cannot tell us how often this WAF would wrongly block a real customer's real users.

That number has never been measured. No amount of detection accuracy substitutes for it, because the failure mode it predicts — legitimate users blocked during business hours — is the one that loses accounts.

The product is built around this honestly rather than around it:

  • Every new site is onboarded in learning mode, which records what it would have blocked without blocking it.
  • fpr-report.sh measures the rate over a chosen window, and the operator manual instructs reading the denominator, not just the percentage.
  • A documented four-point go-live gate must pass before any site is switched to blocking: a full business cycle in learning mode, a measured rate under 1% on real traffic, every recurring detection classified, and application-owner sign-off.
Recommendation The fastest way to close this gap is to put a real ESDS property behind the WAF in learning mode and let it accumulate genuine traffic. That produces the one number the product cannot currently quote — and it is worth more commercially than any feature that could be delivered in the same period.
